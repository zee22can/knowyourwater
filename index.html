The big picture

Follow a drop of water through your watershed

Rain and snowmelt run downhill, fill lakes, soak into the ground and flow along rivers to the ocean. Along the way the sun lifts water back into the air, and the cycle begins again. Every module in the programme starts from this picture.

Learning modules

Six modules, from the tap to the ocean

Each module is short, written for the classroom and tied to one hands-on action.

Module 1

Where our water comes from

Lakes, rivers, groundwater and glaciers. Trace a drop of rain to one of Canada's five ocean basins: Pacific, Arctic, Atlantic, Hudson Bay or Gulf of Mexico.

Hands-on: map your local water source
Module 2

The water cycle and your watershed

Evaporation, transpiration, runoff and storage. Find the watershed your school sits in and name the river or lake it drains to.

Hands-on: build a watershed model
Module 3

Safe drinking water

How water is treated, tested and delivered, and why not every community has had the same access to safe water.

Hands-on: follow water through a treatment process
Module 4

Water and Indigenous knowledge

Water as a relative and a responsibility. Developed with Indigenous advisors and knowledge keepers, so the stories are told by the people they belong to.

Hands-on: learn from local voices
Module 5

Save water at home and school

Showers, taps, leaks, lawns and laundry. Measure what your household uses and set a saving goal.

Hands-on: run a school water check
Module 6

Protect our waters

Stormwater, road salt, plastics and what really goes down the drain. Small habits that keep rivers and lakes clean.

Hands-on: mark a storm drain

School quiz league

Three rounds, one national final

Quizzes run on familiar online platforms such as Kahoot or Quizizz, so a teacher can start in minutes. Each round takes about 15 to 20 minutes, and settings follow each school board's student privacy rules.

In class

Round 1: Classroom quiz

Teacher-led, and every student plays. Questions come from the learning week that came before.

In school

Round 2: School final

Top classes and students from each school compete to name a school champion.

Online, live

Round 3: Inter-school final

School champions meet in a live online final in May or June 2027, with prizes and certificates.

Timeline

What happens, and when

The pilot starts in one province and grows from there.

  1. Nov to Dec 2026

    Foundation Now

    Set goals, pick the pilot province, match modules to its curriculum and recruit advisors, including an Indigenous knowledge keeper.

  2. Jan to Feb 2027

    Content and platform

    Write the six modules in English and French, build the question bank and assemble the teacher kit.

  3. Early March 2027

    Pilot preparation

    Sign up 10 schools, collect parental consent and train teachers in a 30-minute online session.

  4. Mar 22 to May 2027

    Pilot and quiz league

    Launch on World Water Day, then alternate a learning week with a quiz, ending in the online final.

  5. June 2027

    Evaluate

    Compare pre and post quizzes, read teacher and student surveys, and publish a short impact report.

  6. From Sept 2027

    Scale

    Open to more provinces, add the adult and community track, and make the league an annual event.
